Where Ara stands

High at 4K, with the active Discord call: Ara GPU p95 is 3.87 ms, Unity URP 2.14 ms, and Unreal 6.94 ms. Ara uses 44% less GPU time than Unreal and 81% more than Unity. Corresponding 1% lows are 177.7, 321.3, and 114.7 FPS. These are measured effect configurations, not equal-quality renderers.

Unity is the runtime speed leader here, with baked APV rather than dynamic GI and no URP SSR. Ara’s probe lighting and screen-space effects cost less than Unreal’s software Lumen/VSM stack in this scene. These feature/work differences are likely contributors; the benchmark does not isolate their individual costs.

Across the 18 call-condition Ara runs, totaling about six scored minutes, no wall-frame interval exceeded 16 ms. Unity recorded four such intervals and Unreal ten over the corresponding scored windows. That is a result for this static scene and this session, not a general stability guarantee.

Ara High at 1440p has a higher GPU p95 than Epic despite a lower p50. This non-monotonic tail behavior deserves investigation alongside the known preview regressions. The measurements alone do not establish whether CPU stalls, scheduling, power behavior or renderer work causes it.

The corrected Cycles comparison favors Unreal overall: mean FLIP 0.2120 / SSIM 0.9297, versus Ara 0.2199 / 0.9173 and Unity 0.2409 / 0.8972. The small aggregate gap between Ara and Unreal hides obvious local defects: inspect the chrome/copper spheres and lantern. Metrics include tone-map differences and are not an absolute visual-quality score.

Ara loses clearly to Unity on the measured Play-entry proxy in this small scene: about 812 ms versus 81 ms with default domain reload on (93 ms off). Ara observes an embedded Game image; Unity marks its first Game-camera render callback. Unreal’s roughly 58 ms PIE world/next-Slate-tick marker is weaker evidence and must not be presented as a comparable first-rendered-frame result.

Primary condition: with an active Discord call (DWM ~16.4%, Discord ~2.7%).

The engine-free baseline lasted 30.3 s. DWM p50/p95: 16.3/17.6%; Discord p50/p95: 2.6/3.2%. These are the guard’s PDH GPU-engine counter sums across adapters, not an exclusive RTX utilization percentage.

DWM allowance: 22.7% (idle p95 + five percentage points). The foreign sum still rejects >10% sustained for five samples, Discord stays charged, games invalidate on presence, and new GPU process identities above .5% for two samples invalidate the additional check. Chrome receives no GPU allowance.

Runs interleave Ara → Unity → Unreal for each repeat. Three repeats expand to five if any engine’s GPU p50/p95 range exceeds 5% of its median, or wall p95 / 1% low range exceeds 10%. All earlier quiet-admission data is retained separately.

Showcase

One common Blender-authored material/props studio feeds all three engines. It contains 67 rendered mesh objects and 407,603 triangles. The two additional Blender icospheres are importer bone-display helpers in a hidden, non-exported collection; they are not part of the rendered scene.

The material camera shows Ara’s circular black reflection artifacts on the mirror-like spheres and strong lantern transparency differences. Unreal’s reflections are also approximate but closer to Cycles in these views. Unity’s metal spheres mainly reflect the shared environment because URP supplies no SSR. Likely causes include reflection fallback/compositing and material-import support; those are hypotheses, not validated shader diagnoses.

These are static rest meshes and static lights. Dynamic GI is enabled in Ara and Unreal, but this camera path does not test GI response to changing lights or moving geometry. Unity’s successful command-line APV bake supplies indirect lighting with realtime direct lights and shadows.

Specific visual differences: Ara versus Unreal and Cycles

Circular reflection failures

Ara has a nearly black circular hole on the chrome sphere, a dark red/brown disk on the copper sphere, and a flat circular patch on the rough silver sphere. Unreal avoids these large circles, but its chrome center still falls back to a broad gray reflection. Cycles resolves the surrounding props and mannequins. Reflection fallback/compositing is a hypothesis, not a proven shader diagnosis. Materials: Ara 4K · Unreal 4K · Cycles 4K.

Glossy red reflection detail

Ara’s red sphere has a broad, unusually uniform red patch. Unreal shows more surrounding-object reflection structure; Cycles shows clearer silhouettes and a different highlight response. Roughness, screen-space coverage and tonemapping all contribute; the still alone does not isolate the cause. Materials: Ara 4K · Unreal 4K · Cycles 4K.

Lantern glass and metal

Ara’s lantern is pale and ghost-like, including much of its frame and base. Unreal and Cycles retain a dark metal frame with distinct glass regions. This looks like a material/transparency handling difference affecting more than glass tint; the exact import or shader fault is not established. Stage: Ara 4K · Unreal 4K · Cycles 4K.

Translucent sphere appearance

The rear mint and clear spheres look fuller and milkier in Ara. Unreal and Cycles reveal more of the background through these spheres. Unreal also has visible faceting and does not reproduce the Cycles transmission exactly. Materials: Ara 4K · Unreal 4K · Cycles 4K.

Cast-shadow edges and grounding

The main cast-shadow direction agrees across the views. Ara’s long floor shadows have relatively hard, uniform edges; Cycles has more gradual penumbrae. Unreal has stronger darkening near some contacts. This is a visible shadow/filtering difference, not evidence of an incorrect sun angle. Stage: Ara 4K · Unreal 4K · Cycles 4K.

Contact darkening and indirect-light gradients

Ara has less darkening around several pedestal bases and prop contacts than Unreal, and its pedestal sides read more uniformly. Cycles shows more localized contact shading without Unreal’s broad darkening. These images combine GI, AO and shadow terms, so they cannot attribute the difference to one subsystem or rank dynamic GI response. Props: Ara 4K · Unreal 4K · Cycles 4K.

Diagonal shading on flat pedestals

Ara shows broad diagonal tonal patches across otherwise flat white pedestal faces, especially the tripod pedestal. They are much less apparent in Unreal and Cycles. Geometry, normals and lighting/compositing would need separate inspection to identify the cause. Props: Ara 4K · Unreal 4K · Cycles 4K.

Overall tone and background

Ara’s background is lighter than Unreal and Cycles, with different floor and white-pedestal contrast despite the fixed common EV. Filmic/ACES/AgX and approximate sky/lighting responses differ. No exposure fitting was used to hide these differences. Stage: Ara 4K · Unreal 4K · Cycles 4K.

Method, provenance and limits

Windows 11, RTX 4070 SUPER, Ryzen 7 7800X3D, approximately 31 GB RAM. One engine runs at a time. Every timing batch has an nvidia-smi process/load check plus a game/OBS process scan, then runs under cargo run -p ara-dev -- gpu -- from the required Ara repository. GPU-guard records, per-run process ownership and raw measurements are retained under D:\AraBench\logs and measurements. Only owned processes are allowed to be stopped.

The monitors are physically 2560×1440. 4K uses oversized borderless windows with verified 3840×2160 render/capture surfaces, clipped by the desktop. It is a 4K rendering workload, not 4K physical scanout. Capture runs are separate from timing. Encoded videos contain fixed-step frames; their playback rate is not a claim of measured realtime 60 FPS.

Ara uses a git-archive snapshot of 13e8b9da and isolated scratch configuration/cache directories. The benchmark adapter changes camera input, capture/timing instrumentation and window presentation only; it does not change rendering algorithms. The original source checkout is retained unchanged. Engine builds and repeated calibration errors are recorded; rejected captures are kept outside the accepted measurement paths.

Warm steady-state results exclude startup. Ara waits for scene readiness, then 180 frames; Unity also warms 180 frames. Unreal CSV excludes its first five seconds and scores one complete 20-second period of the looping shared path. Three or five per-run statistics are reduced by median, using the variance rule above. GPU clocks and temperature are logged, with no manual overclock changes. Filesystem and driver caches are not reset or cold-boot controlled.

This preview is known to have cold pipeline and first-lit-frame regressions. The table reports this binary’s measured behavior. Low/Medium presets and historical alpha comparisons were not run, so no result here establishes that those regressions are fixed.

Raw summaries, source manifests, adapters, original images and encoded media are retained in D:\AraBench. This page is generated from those summaries; it uses no JavaScript or external image dependencies. Video links point to separately published media.

Quiet-admission results remain available as requested. The earliest Ara/Unity camera adapters updated at 60 Hz while Unreal interpolated. Their numerical results are valid for those recorded configurations, but the primary interleaved call-condition matrix corrects this mismatch. Additional accepted continuous-camera Ara quiet runs are also retained separately.

An extra post-run process check initially treated a pre-existing Chrome process as new because it had no GPU activity in the idle samples. Its baseline presence and process birth time established that it was not new. The check was corrected; Chrome received no allowance and stayed charged by the native foreign-load threshold. No performance outlier was deleted for this correction.

Actual first display presentation is not measured for all three engines: PresentMon ETW access was denied. Ara exposes player present/readiness markers, Unity provides a first-scene CPU callback, and Unreal provides an initialization timestamp. These different startup markers are labeled separately rather than used for a startup winner.

All timing executables remain immutable. Offline video capture has separate readback instrumentation, fixed 60 Hz camera samples, and no renderer algorithm changes. Captures are excluded from performance tables. The original Ara checkout has no benchmark implementation edits; its isolated source snapshot and adapters are retained under the work directory.

The Unreal Showcase movie completed its 1,200-frame success callback and both encodes independently decoded as 20 seconds at 60 Hz. The editor then exited with access-violation status during shutdown; this capture failure is retained in the session log. Offline capture is excluded from timing results.

Outpost source preparation began before cancellation, but produced no accepted cross-engine measurements or reference images. Hangar was not started. No Outpost or Hangar result is included in this final report.

Snist media has a 25 MB upload limit. Published 4K delivery videos use 8 Mbit/s HEVC; the higher-quality CQ18 4K originals and H.264 1080p copies remain under D:\AraBench\report\videos. Both versions retain 1,200 frames at 60 Hz.
