Sun shadows: heightfield horizon and virtual shadow maps

D27 raster look · Outpost · 2026-10-26 · horizon in batch 32 (1718a289, 08943472); virtual shadow maps in batch 35 (4e0b8f17)

Distant terrain is now shadowed by a heightfield horizon on every preset. High and Epic also get Unreal-style virtual shadow maps for the sun. Against 256-sample path-traced references, eight of ten Outpost views improve on High, and pages arrive without visible pops on a 20 m/s approach. High costs 0.09 to 0.10 ms more (p50 3.40 to 3.50 ms, same session). Low and Medium pay nothing.

Decisions for you: none. Two planned extras, soft (SMRT-style) and contact shadows, were built and measured, and are not shipped. Neither improved any view, both cost time, and both added artifacts (see below). Open gaps are listed last.

What was built

  • Heightfield horizon (all presets). A top-down capture of terrain and large solid casters becomes a heightfield. A compute pass marches each texel toward the sun for its visible share, and twelve azimuths give its sky visibility. Shading takes the darker of the sun maps and the horizon, so mountains shadow terrain far beyond the cascades. Outside the probe volume, distant landforms also occlude the sky light.
  • Virtual shadow maps (High, Epic). Sixteen clipmap levels of 16k virtual texels (level n covers a radius of 2n m) are split into 128-texel pages that stay cached in world space. VSM.Mark records the pages visible pixels need (one pixel per 4×4 block, wave-coalesced); the marks are read back once their frame completes and drive a deterministic LRU page cache. Dirty pages render under a per-frame budget (32 on High, 64 on Epic); moved casters re-dirty only the pages they touch. VSM.Project resolves each pixel's page visibility into a mask lighting reads. GI relight compares its probe hits against pages in its own pipeline.
  • No cost on Low and Medium. The first version inlined page lookups in the shared lighting and GI shaders, which cost Low and Medium about 0.06 ms each through register pressure alone (live A/B). The projection pass and the separate GI pipeline remove that: those presets measure at their old numbers.

GI no longer leaks sunlight into the hangar

Probe GI shaded its rays' hits with the coarse sun maps, whose texels and normal offset let probes under the thin hangar roof read as sunlit. Comparing those hits against pages keeps the interior dark, as in the reference. That is most of approach 0's gain (0.145 to 0.134).

The sun's angular radius is about 0.27 degrees, so a 5 m blocker casts a penumbra of about 5 cm, roughly three pixels at yard distances. The 3×3 filter already gives that. Wide penumbrae come only from tall blockers such as cliffs and mountains, and they fall on distant receivers, where coarser page texels make the filter about as wide. Unreal needs ray-marched soft shadows because its pages are sampled unfiltered; ours are filtered.

Known gaps

  • Dusk ambient deficit. Shadowed ground renders about 25% darker than the path tracer across Outpost at dusk (for example 52 against 72 out of 255 in the east view's foreground). This predates the shadow work and is the largest remaining FLIP term. It is a look item (sky and GI energy), queued after the TAAU motion work.
  • East mountains, +0.004. Attributed: the horizon's sun term, applied to off-screen probe hits no page resolves, darkens the ground behind the east fence slightly more than the reference. Dropping it there fixes the east view but regresses the editor and approach views by as much, so it stays as is.
