Ara Fidelity: exceeding UE5, not imitating it

Research report and draft decision D29 for Islam · 2026-10-27 · research sub-agent (no engine code changed, no GPU runs) · evidence: the cross-engine benchmark (RTX 4070 SUPER, Showcase, UE 5.7.4 software Lumen, Unity 6.6 URP, Blender Cycles reference), the Mac Metal trace, the D26 regression notes, the renderer sources at 13e8b9da, and the primary sources listed at the end.

The short answer

  • Where we stand. On Showcase, Ara uses 1.8–2.9× less GPU time than UE 5.7, and its overall FLIP error against Cycles is within 4% of Unreal's (0.220 vs 0.212). It loses where screen space and raster run out: mirror reflections, glass, soft sun shadows and near-field indirect light. Unity URP is about 2× faster than Ara. Part of that is less work (baked GI, no SSR); part is structural overhead we already know how to remove.
  • The worst defects have known causes. The black discs come from a screen-space reflection (SSR) bug: rays aimed back toward the camera hit their own pixel. The ghostly lantern comes from transmission ignoring metallic. The milky glass has two causes: the Showcase export writes alpha blending instead of transmission, and Ara has no refraction. These are days of work, not missing technology.
  • What UE5 has that we don't: reflections and GI traced with hardware rays against a lighting cache (Lumen HWRT); soft shadows that sharpen near the contact point (SMRT); a layered material model (Substrate); many-light shading (MegaLights, production-ready in 5.8); translucency lighting; volumetric clouds and volumes; hair strands; and a mature Nanite (our version is in progress).
  • How to beat it rather than copy it. I call the approach "deterministic hybrid". Hardware rays answer exact questions: a mirror ray, a refraction ray, a shadow ray, a cache update. World-space caches do the integration, so there is no per-pixel noise. Screen space adds detail, and the best upscaler on each GPU reconstructs the final image. This targets UE's documented weaknesses (noise, ghosting, lag, leaking, glass) and fits D27's no-noise spirit.
  • Honest 10×. We can't get 10× across the whole frame against UE's own presets. UE Epic takes 6.25 ms at 1440p, so 10× would be 0.63 ms, below what Unity with baked GI already needs (1.09 ms). Realistic targets: UE-Epic quality at 35–40% of its GPU time (Ara High: 2.5 vs 6.25 ms at 1440p, 4.0 vs 11.7 ms at 4K), and visibly better than UE Epic at 55–65% of it (Ara Epic). A 10× gain is real for individual subsystems (GI update, many-light shadows, cached shadows), and for presented frame rate once ML upscaling and frame generation are counted. Those count separately from D26.
  • Your decision: D29 (draft at the end). It would replace D27's "raster on every GPU" and "hardware RT later" clauses and keep D27's no-noise rule, budgets and record targets. E0–E3 and the immediate wins need no decision and can start now.

What the images show

Mirror reflections. Cycles reflects the stage behind the camera. Ara shows a black disc on chrome and a dark-red disc on copper, and the same disc appears on every glossy sphere and on the bottle. The disc is a bug, diagnosed in §8. Outside the disc, Ara has nothing to reflect off screen except the sky: it has no local reflection captures and no rays. UE fills the region with a blurry approximation from Lumen's surface cache. Unity reflects only the environment map.

Lantern. The brass material in Lantern_01_2k.gltf carries KHR_materials_transmission with factor 1, but its metallic texture marks it as metal. Under glTF's BSDF, metal never transmits. Ara's forward pass sets alpha to alpha·(1−transmission) and never looks at metallic, so the whole lantern disappears. confirmed from asset + shader Unity mishandles it too. The distant spheres in this crop show the disc bug at a smaller scale.

Glass. In Cycles the clear sphere refracts the floor and the mint sphere is frosted glass. The exported ball_glass.gltf is alphaMode: BLEND: base colour 0.92 white at alpha 0.12 (the tinted ball is at 0.35), with no transmission, IOR or volume. Every engine therefore draws a whitish film. Ara has no refraction, absorption or thickness model either, and imports only the transmission and emissive_strength glTF extensions. UE shows faceted silhouettes and flat transparency. Unreal's raster translucency has no rough refraction outside its path tracer, so this is a place to beat it outright.

Sun shadows. Cycles' sun has an angular size, so the penumbra widens with distance from the occluder: sharp at the contact, soft at the far tip. Ara's PCF uses a fixed kernel, so the edge is uniform. UE's SMRT approximates contact hardening. Unity blurs everything.

Near-field indirect light. Cycles shows warm floor bounce rising up the pedestal sides and tight darkening at the base. Ara's side faces are flatter, with weaker contact darkening and broad diagonal tonal patches on some faces (see the benchmark's visual findings). Despite that, Ara scores best here: UE over-darkens the contacts. Half-resolution GTAO with a 1 m radius, plus a probe lattice, cannot resolve occlusion and bounce at the 10–50 cm scale.

Dark metals. Ara's black rubber and dark metal are crushed compared with Cycles and UE: they get less floor bounce on undersides and less glossy lift. Likely causes, none confirmed: no multiple-scattering energy compensation on rough metal, specular occlusion stacked on probe occlusion, and the display curve.

Metal bottle. The same SSR self-hit appears as a dark vertical patch, and it reduces the legibility of the printed logo.

5. Proposed Ara Fidelity architecture

Principle: exact rays, cached integrals, one stable signal.

  1. Rays answer deterministic questions: a mirror or near-mirror reflection, a refraction, a shadow test, a cache update. One fixed ray per pixel on a smooth surface has no Monte Carlo noise.
  2. Integrals live in world-space caches that converge over frames and are shared by every pixel: a radiance hash cache, irradiance probes and reflection captures. Per pixel, the cost is a lookup.
  3. Screen space adds detail (HZB SSR, bitmask GI, contact shadows). Reconstruction comes last: TAAU or a vendor upscaler.

8. Immediate wins

8.1 The reflection discs: diagnosis and fix from the code not yet GPU-verified

In shaders/look-ssr.slang, ssrTraceMain projects the ray's end point world + r·reach with reach = min(maxDistance, 2·cameraDistance). When the reflected ray points back toward the camera, that end point lies behind the camera. Its projection has w < 0, so its screen position is mirrored and its reversed-Z depth is negative. The guard if(b.z<=0) then interpolates in NDC toward depth 0. In reversed-Z, depth 0 is infinitely far away, not the near plane. The march therefore starts heading "away" from the camera. At the very first step its depth is behind the sphere's own surface, so the binary search settles on the start pixel. The thickness test passes, and SSR reports a confident hit on the pixel itself. Shading then takes last frame's colour at that same pixel, times the specular albedo, every frame. That feedback converges to black on chrome, dark red on copper and gold, and a dimmer patch on rougher metals. The set of normals whose reflected ray is long enough to pass behind the camera forms a cap, which projects to a disc. The sun highlight survives inside the disc because it is added outside SSR, which matches the Ara crop.

// Fix sketch for ssrTraceMain. Replace the NDC "b.z<=0" lerp: clip in view space (McGuire & Mara 2014 [44]).
float3 forward = cameraForward(f);                       // from f.inverseVp
float depth0   = dot(world.xyz - f.camera.xyz, forward); // view depth of the start
float along    = dot(r, forward);
if (along < 0) reach = min(reach, 0.9 * (depth0 - nearDistance(f)) / -along);
// While marching: ignore "hits" within the first step's pixel span (self-intersection),
// and fade confidence as dot(r, -v) rises toward 1 (rays facing the camera have no data on screen).

Confirm it first with the SSR debug output: disc pixels should report confidence ≈ 1 and a hit UV equal to their own. After the fix the discs become the probe/sky fallback, similar to UE's grey centre. Reflection captures (E1b) or one RT ray (E1c) then add the stage. The fix has no cost.

8.2 The lantern confirmed

Multiply transmission by (1 − metallic) where the surface is built (shaders/native-raster.slang:106), so diffuse, alpha (look-shading.slangh:245) and the BRDF all agree. Do it per texel: the metallic texture decides metal versus glass inside one material. glTF's KHR_materials_transmission mixes transmission only into the dielectric part [47]. Also check that importer routing doesn't send a mostly-metal material to the forward pass.

8.3 Glass

  • Content: the Showcase exporter must write KHR_materials_transmission, ior and volume (thickness, attenuation) instead of alpha blending.
  • Import: add KHR_materials_ior, volume and specular, then clearcoat, anisotropy and sheen for Substrate-class coverage.
  • Render: screen-space refraction from the existing SSR colour pyramid (roughness picks the level, thickness scales the offset), Beer–Lambert absorption and two-interface Fresnel. Bind only HDR and depth in the forward pass. On the hybrid tier, add an RT refraction ray.

11. Draft decision D29 for Islam; decisions.md not edited

IDDecisionDate
D29Ara Fidelity: deterministic hybrid rendering for High and Epic (supersedes D27's "every preset is raster on every GPU" and "hardware RT becomes a later milestone" clauses; D27's budgets, record targets and no-visible-noise rule stay, and A8's baked/probe fallback stays mandatory).
• Low and Medium stay raster on every GPU. High and Epic use hardware ray queries on GPUs that have them (RTX 20+, RX 6000+, Arc, Apple M3+). Other GPUs fall back to the raster High/Epic path. Both paths are first-class and gated.
• Rays answer deterministic questions: mirror and glossy reflections below a roughness cut, refraction, shadow visibility and world-space cache updates. Lighting integrals live in world-space caches (radiance hash cache, irradiance probes, reflection captures), and pixels look them up. A per-pixel stochastic pass (an Epic final gather, many-light resampling) ships only if it passes the stability gate.
• "No visible noise" becomes a measured gate: a temporal-stability metric (static camera and slow pan, per 16×16 block), reported per preset beside timings.
• Quality gate: region FLIP against a matched-transform Cycles reference. High is no worse than the latest UE 5.x Epic in any region. Epic beats UE Epic's mean by at least 15%.
• Showcase-class record targets on the RTX 4070 SUPER: High 2.5 ms and Epic 4.0 ms at 1440p (4.0 / 6.5 ms at 4K). Outpost-class keeps D27's 3.0 / 5.0 ms. M5 Pro targets are set from its first measurement.
• Vendor reconstruction (DLSS, XeSS, FSR, MetalFX) and frame generation are optional runtime components, diagnosed like the Steam Audio runtime. Ara TAAU stays the default, the reference and the fallback. Generated frames never count toward D26/D27 budgets.		proposed 2026-10-27

Why it supersedes part of D27. D27's mechanism has become the measured ceiling:

  • Screen space can't fix the biggest gaps. Off-screen reflections and refraction cannot be solved in screen space; reflection captures only fix static scenes.
  • RT hardware is now the common case. Roughly 79% of Steam GPUs have it (inferred from the 21% without RT hardware [46]), as does every Apple M3+.
  • RT GI already ships cheap and stable. id Tech 8 ships RT-required GI in ~2 ms at 60 Hz down to Series S [15].
  • UE has moved on. Its flagship path is now hardware-ray-traced Lumen at 60 fps on PS5 [13][14]. Staying raster-only caps our High/Epic quality below UE's.

D27's goal, no visible noise, is kept and made stricter, because it becomes a measured gate instead of a rule about technique.

Open decisions

  1. D29: adopt as drafted, adopt without the Epic per-pixel gather, or keep D27 and accept the raster ceiling (reflection captures plus SSR plus probes).
  2. Vendor SDKs as optional runtime components (DLSS/Streamline, XeSS, FSR, MetalFX). They don't change the build prerequisites, but they need a licence review while D14 is open.
  3. How we talk about 10×: I recommend "UE-Epic quality at 35–40% of the GPU time" as the headline, with frame generation reported separately.
  4. Start now (no decision needed): E0, E1a, the lantern fix, E2 and E3.
